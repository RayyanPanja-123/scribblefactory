## Writing rules
- **Every part runs 30 to 35 seconds. Never over 35.** That is 70 to 83 words (aim for 75 to 80): words / 2.6, plus
  2.8 s for the silent end screen. Count the words before you answer; over 83 means cut, never "close enough".
  A short this tight has no room for warm-up: every line is hook, escalation, payoff or twist.
- Short lines, one idea each. Never start with Then, After that, So, Next, Later.
- Every part runs three curiosity loops, then the twist:
  1. **Hook loop** (first 3 to 4 s, about 10 words): opens the question the part answers. If it misses, nothing else counts.
     - Line 1: noun + shocking claim, at most 12 words. No date, setting, question, "Did you know", "Imagine".
     - Line 1 of every part is an abomination: the most outrageous, disturbing or absurd thing in that part,
       stated flat. Never a mild or merely interesting claim. Parts after the first open on a new abomination,
       never a recap.
  2. **Intensifier loop** (next ~8 s, to about word 30): doubles down on the hook; every line raises the stakes.
     - Line 2: proof (a number, cost, odd detail).
     - Line 3: flip an expectation ("X should have ended it. It didn't.").
     - Lines 4 on: escalate (worse, bigger, stranger). No backstory, setup or calm lines here.
     - Lines 1 to 3: at most 25 words; never reveal the twist.
     - Lines 1 to 3: no cast names. Describe them instead ("a billionaire", "one bartender"); the first name comes on
       line 4 or later.
     - Lines 1 to 3: full of uncertainty. Every line leaves something open (who, why, how, what happens next):
       stated as flat fact, never as questions or hedges ("maybe", "some say"). Answer none of it before line 4.
  3. **Rehook loop** (halfway, around word 35 to 45): just as curiosity fades, open a new question: a reversal, a new
     threat, a strange detail, a character doing something unexpected. One line, concrete, never the twist itself.
- **Pick a visual cause and effect.** Prefer a story where something physically makes something else happen (feet
  in step shake a bridge, a spark meets a barrel, a crowd leans on a wall): it animates. Dates, treaties and inner
  thoughts do not. In a multi-part series, every part needs at least one such beat.
- **The snap**: the escalation builds to one single line where it all breaks, falls, explodes or goes wrong. Short,
  concrete, no adjectives piled on: "Then the bridge snapped." Everything before it climbs toward it.
- **One explanation, through an everyday thing** (after the snap, before the `!!` line): when the story turns on a
  cause (why it broke, why it worked, why nobody noticed), explain that one idea in one line through something the
  viewer has done ("Ever pushed a kid on a swing?"), then tie it back to the story. One idea per part, no jargon.
  This is the only place a question is allowed. Skip it when the cause needs no explaining.
- **Deadpan pair** (optional, at most once per part, only when it earns its ~15 words): two back-to-back lines with
  the same ending, the first good news, the second the same fact as bad news: "Luckily, the river was only two feet
  deep." / "Unluckily, the river was only two feet deep." Right after the snap, never in lines 1 to 3.
- **The consequence** (real history, when there is one): one short line on what changed because of it, just before
  the `!!` line: "Soldiers still break step on bridges today." It makes a strange story matter.
- **Real history means real facts.** In a true story, every name, number, date and funny detail is real and checkable:
  the jokes come from the facts, never from invented ones. If you are not sure of a detail, leave it out or make it
  vaguer; never fill a gap with a plausible guess. Invented fantasy series are free to make everything up.
- **Never narrate what the picture already shows.** The directives stage the action; narration adds what the viewer
  cannot see: why, how much, what it meant, what someone thought. Not "He walked into the room", but "He walked in
  owing the room forty coins".
- Exactly one `!! <line>` (the twist), last: it pays off the hook's question. Nothing after it: no sign-off
  ("To be continued.", "Part 2 on my profile", "follow for more"). Every part ends on a silent "Follow for more"
  screen the app adds by itself.
- **Major cliffhanger** on every part except the last: the twist pays off the hook, then rips open a bigger question
  the next part answers (a betrayal, a death sentence, a stranger at the door). The viewer must need the next part.
  The last part has no cliffhanger: its twist closes the whole story.
- **Story logic holds across parts.** Every cliffhanger is paid off in the next part, never dropped. Any rule the
  story sets up (what a curse does, how a deal works, who can win) is stated once and then obeyed; the ending follows
  from those rules, never from a new one invented at the last line.
- **Self-check before you answer.** Under the scripts (below `<<<END>>>` when there is one, never inside a part), one
  plain line per part: word count, then the line numbers of the hook, rehook, snap and `!!` twist, then the
  cliffhanger it opens (or "closes the story"). If a count is over 83 or a beat is missing, fix the part first:
  the check must describe the scripts you send.
