## Writing rules
- 110 to 175 words (aim under 145); at most 58 s (words / 2.6, plus 2.8 s for the silent end screen).
- Short lines, one idea each. Never start with Then, After that, So, Next, Later.
- Every part runs three curiosity loops, then the twist:
  1. **Hook loop** (first 5 s, about 13 words): opens the question the part answers. If it misses, nothing else counts.
     - Line 1: noun + shocking claim, at most 12 words. No date, setting, question, "Did you know", "Imagine".
     - Line 1 of every part is an abomination: the most outrageous, disturbing or absurd thing in that part,
       stated flat. Never a mild or merely interesting claim. Parts after the first open on a new abomination,
       never a recap.
  2. **Intensifier loop** (next ~15 s, to about word 50): doubles down on the hook; every line raises the stakes.
     - Line 2: proof (a number, cost, odd detail).
     - Line 3: flip an expectation ("X should have ended it. It didn't.").
     - Lines 4 on: escalate (worse, bigger, stranger). No backstory, setup or calm lines here.
     - Lines 1 to 3: at most 30 words; never reveal the twist.
     - Lines 1 to 3: no cast names. Describe them instead ("a billionaire", "one bartender"); the first name comes on
       line 4 or later.
     - Lines 1 to 3: full of uncertainty. Every line leaves something open (who, why, how, what happens next):
       stated as flat fact, never as questions or hedges ("maybe", "some say"). Answer none of it before line 4.
  3. **Rehook loop** (halfway, around word 55 to 75): just as curiosity fades, open a new question: a reversal, a new
     threat, a strange detail, a character doing something unexpected. One line, concrete, never the twist itself.
- **One explanation, through an everyday thing** (line 4 or later, before the `!!` line): when the story turns on a
  cause (why it broke, why it worked, why nobody noticed), explain that one idea in one or two lines through something
  the viewer has done ("You see, ..." or "Ever pushed a kid on a swing?"), then tie it back to the story. One idea per
  part, no jargon unless the same line explains it. This is the only place a question is allowed.
- **Deadpan pair** (optional, at most once per part): two back-to-back lines with the same ending, the first good news,
  the second the same fact as bad news: "Luckily, the river was only two feet deep." / "Unluckily, the river was only
  two feet deep." Best right after the big beat breaks, never in lines 1 to 3.
- **Never narrate what the picture already shows.** The directives stage the action; narration adds what the viewer
  cannot see: why, how much, what it meant, what someone thought. Not "He walked into the room", but "He walked in
  owing the room forty coins".
- Exactly one `!! <line>` (the twist), last: it pays off the hook's question. Nothing after it: no sign-off
  ("To be continued.", "Part 2 on my profile", "follow for more"). Every part ends on a silent "Follow for more"
  screen the app adds by itself.
- **Major cliffhanger** on every part except the last: the twist pays off the hook, then rips open a bigger question
  the next part answers (a betrayal, a death sentence, a stranger at the door). The viewer must need the next part.
  The last part has no cliffhanger: its twist closes the whole story.
