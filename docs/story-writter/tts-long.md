## Writing rules: long video (16:9 series, one part = a chapter of several minutes)
For a 16:9 series. A 9:16 series uses tts-shorts.md instead. The voice mode rules (tts.md) apply to both.
- **Length: the creator's minutes per part, never over.** Speech runs about 2.3 words a second: 1 min is 135 to 140 words, so minutes x 135 is the target and minutes x 140 the ceiling. Count before answering; over = cut.
- Short lines, one idea each (a line is one TTS clip). Never start with Then, After that, So, Next, Later. Numbers as words.
- **Talk to the viewer like a friend**: an aside ("you", "picture this") every 6 to 10 lines, never on line 1.
- **The title is its own `# ` line, right after the marker.** Line 1 is the hook, never a title, headline or "The Battle of X:" label.
- **Hook** (line 1, at most 12 words): noun + the most outrageous claim of the chapter, stated flat. No date, question, "Did you know". Lines 1 to 3: at most 25 words, no cast names, each leaves something open.
- **Lines 2 and 3 never give the story away**: they prove the hook and flip an expectation, and each leaves something open. The payoff waits for the `!!`.
- **No hedging filler.** Never "reportedly", "supposedly", "allegedly", "according to the traditional account" more than once per chapter. State the claim flat, or leave it out. A famous legend that real sources dispute: say so once, in one line ("Historians doubt this part."), then tell only what holds. Never pad a chapter with disputed numbers.
- **Cast lines are 3 to 12 words and never answer each other with repeats**: no "Who fired?" / "Identify the enemy!" pairs. Each speaker brings a different fact, joke or fear, in their own voice. Never three cast lines in a row; a narrator line sits between.
- **Cast lines add something new** (a joke, a jab, a reveal, a reaction to what just happened), never restate the narrator's line before or after. If a cast line could be cut and nothing is lost, cut it.
- **Beats, not one arc.** Split the chapter into beats of about 25 to 35 s (70 to 80 words). Each beat: a claim, proof (number, cost, odd detail), a flip ("X should have ended it. It didn't."), one concrete snap. A beat ends on a fresh open question that the next beat answers: that is the rehook. Check every beat's last line: if it closes the question instead of opening one, rewrite it.
- **Breathe**: after every 3 or 4 beats one calm line (a consequence, an everyday comparison) so the chapter is not one long shout. Never a recap.
- **Visual cause and effect**: every beat has a physical cause when one exists. Dates, treaties, thoughts do not animate.
- **Real history = real facts**: every name, number, date is checkable; unsure = leave out. Invented fantasy may make things up. Never narrate what the picture shows; add why, how much, what it meant.
- **Exactly one `!! <line>`**, last in the chapter, paying off the hook. Nothing after it: no sign-off or "follow for more".
- **Cliffhanger** on every chapter but the last: the twist opens a bigger question the next chapter answers. The last chapter closes the story. Logic holds across chapters.
- **Self-check** below `<<<END>>>` (never inside a part), one line per part: word count against the ceiling, number of beats, line number of the `!!`, the cliffhanger it opens (or "closes the story").
