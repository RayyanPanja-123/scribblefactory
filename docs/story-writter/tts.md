## Voice mode (settled with the creator before any script)
- **Single narration**: every line is the narrator's.
- **Cast voice acting**: narrator plus cast. A cast line is `<cast id>: words` (lowercase id, e.g. `kai: Everyone alive?`); all other lines are the narrator's. One line, one voice; never two at once.
  - Cast lines: 3 to 12 words, in character, each does work (joke, reaction, jab, reveal); never repeat the narrator.
  - Lines 1 to 3 stay narration. After that, a third to a half of the lines spoken by the cast; two or three in a row is fine.
  - The twist may be spoken: `!! kai: Then we walk.`
  - Same id for a speaker every time; their words count toward the word limit.
  - The narrator talks to the viewer; the cast talk to each other, never to the viewer.

## Writing rules
- **Length: 30 to 35 s, never over.** That is 70 to 83 words (aim 75 to 80). Count before answering; over 83 = cut.
- Short lines, one idea each. Never start with Then, After that, So, Next, Later.
- **Talk to the viewer like a friend.** From line 2 on, 2 to 3 asides per part ("you", "picture this", "guess what he did next"): 2 to 5 words inside a line, as statements, never on line 1.
- **Spoken bridges** for causes, rules or conditions: "You see, ...", "Here's the catch: ...", "That's because ...", "Which meant ...". One per explained idea, comma after it.
- **Three loops, then the twist:**
  - **Hook** (line 1, at most 12 words): noun + the most outrageous, absurd or disturbing claim of the part, stated flat. No date, setting, question, "Did you know", "Imagine". Later parts open on a new abomination, never a recap.
  - **Intensifier** (to about word 30): line 2 = proof (number, cost, odd detail); line 3 = flip an expectation ("X should have ended it. It didn't."); line 4 on = escalate. No backstory or calm lines.
    - Lines 1 to 3: at most 25 words, no cast names (describe: "a billionaire"), flat statements that each leave something open, never reveal the twist.
  - **Rehook** (word 35 to 45): one concrete line opening a new question (reversal, new threat, odd detail). Never the twist.
- **Visual cause and effect**: prefer physical causes (feet in step shake a bridge). Every part has at least one such beat. Dates, treaties, thoughts do not animate.
- **The snap**: escalation climbs to one short, concrete line where it breaks ("Then the bridge snapped.").
- **One explanation** (after the snap, before `!!`, only if the cause needs it): one line through an everyday thing ("Ever pushed a kid on a swing?"), then a bridge back ("You see, the bridge was that swing."). The only question allowed.
- **Deadpan pair** (optional, once per part, right after the snap): "Luckily, the river was only two feet deep." / "Unluckily, the river was only two feet deep."
- **Consequence** (real history, when there is one): one short line before `!!` on what changed.
- **Real history = real facts**: every name, number, date, detail is checkable; unsure = leave out. Invented fantasy may make things up.
- **Never narrate what the picture shows**; add why, how much, what it meant. Not "He walked in", but "He walked in owing the room forty coins".
- **Exactly one `!! <line>`**, last, paying off the hook. Nothing after it: no sign-off or "follow for more" (the app adds the end screen).
- **Cliffhanger** on every part but the last: the twist pays off the hook, then opens a bigger question the next part answers. The last part closes the story.
- **Logic holds across parts**: every cliffhanger is paid off next part; a rule is stated once, then obeyed; the ending follows from the rules.
- **Self-check** below `<<<END>>>` (never inside a part), one line per part: word count, line numbers of hook, rehook, snap, `!!`, then the cliffhanger it opens (or "closes the story"). Fix any part over 83 words or missing a beat first.
